## Candidate Scorecards

### A — TrustText

**What it does:** Missed-call text-back and background check / certification expiry reminder SMS for home care and childcare staffing agencies. AI monitors caregiver certification expiration dates (background checks, CPR/first aid, state licenses) and triggers renewal reminder sequences (30/7/1 day before); missed inquiry call from a prospective family triggers immediate SMS follow-up and lead capture.

**Evidence:**
- Home care placement agencies and childcare staffing agencies are private-pay B2B businesses managing compliance for 5–100+ caregivers per location. Background check and first-aid certifications expire on 12–24 month cycles; expired credentials = liability, state audit risk, potential license suspension.
- Current tooling: spreadsheets and manual calendar reminders. No purpose-built SMS compliance-alert tool exists for the caregiver staffing niche.
- Market: 33,000+ licensed home health agencies + 12,000+ childcare and nanny placement agencies in the US — large addressable market.
- Reachability: NAHC (National Association for Home Care & Hospice), INA (International Nanny Association), NAPNES, state home care association directories — organized, accessible peer channels.
- "Safe connection" fit: trust verification between vulnerable families and the workers entering their homes is the literal value proposition. Compliance = maintained trust.
- Dual trigger: (1) missed_call from family inquiring about caregiver placement; (2) reminder_due for background check renewal or certification expiry (recurring compliance event, not a visit appointment).
- TCPA: caregiver employment agreements include communication consent; client intake forms cover family contact.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **7.5** — medium/high confidence. $99–149/mo is trivial vs. compliance liability. 45,000+ potential buyers nationally. No dominant purpose-built compliance SMS competitor in the caregiver staffing niche.
- Popularity Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. NAHC and INA networks are organized and directory-accessible. Compliance pain is universal among agency owners → word-of-mouth within peer groups; "we use AI to track every background check expiry" is a strong referral hook.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **7.5** — high confidence. Background check renewal = scheduled reminder event; missed inquiry = standard missed_call trigger; RENEW/CONFIRM/STOP keywords; no clinical integration; onboard in under 10 minutes.

**Composite:** (7.5 × 0.40) + (7.0 × 0.35) + (7.5 × 0.25) = 3.000 + 2.450 + 1.875 = **7.325**

### B — SafeText

**What it does:** Safe child release authorization and pickup confirmation SMS for youth sports clubs, after-school programs, and summer camps. Parent pre-authorizes pickup person via SMS; at pickup time, staff sends a CONFIRM request and receives an authorized CONFIRM or DENY code before releasing the child.

**Evidence:**
- Youth sports clubs, after-school programs, and summer camps have real liability around child release authorization — releasing to an unauthorized adult is an actionable incident. Paper sign-out sheets and verbal confirmations are the current norm; SMS authorization creates a digital audit trail.
- Market: 70,000+ registered youth sports organizations; 22,000+ licensed after-school programs; 12,000+ ACA-accredited summer camps.
- Structural barrier: most youth sports organizations are volunteer-run with minimal administrative budgets. After-school programs are frequently nonprofit. Commercial summer camps ($500–2,000/camper/session) have the budget and liability sensitivity to pay.
- Pricing sensitivity: $99/mo is difficult for volunteer-run leagues. Decision-maker is often a volunteer board without purchasing authority, making sales cycles long and unpredictable.
- Reachability: ACA (American Camp Association), NAYS (National Alliance for Youth Sports) — directories exist but buyer pool is fragmented across thousands of independent orgs with no central purchasing authority.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **6.5** — medium confidence. Commercial camps can pay; volunteer youth leagues are budget-constrained. Decision-maker fragmentation (volunteer boards vs. owner-operators) depresses sales velocity and revenue ceiling.
- Popularity Potential: **6.5** — medium confidence. ACA and NAYS networks are reachable but fragmented. Word-of-mouth is limited by the fact that most orgs are volunteer-run and have no marketing function; peer referral is slow.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **8.0** — high confidence. Pickup = scheduled event; CONFIRM/DENY/STOP keywords; missed_call = inquiry from new family; architecture is identical to prior winners; onboard under 10 minutes.

### C — WatchText

**What it does:** Daily AI wellness check-in SMS for home care agency clients — automated morning check-in ("Good morning, how are you feeling today?") with SAFE/HELP response keywords; no-response or HELP triggers automated escalation alert to the care coordinator. AI analyzes response patterns over time and flags anomalies (unusual delay, missed consecutive check-ins) before they become emergencies. Missed inquiry from an adult child researching home care → immediate SMS follow-up.

*Note: WatchText is distinct from CareText (built, Day 14). CareText handles care visit scheduling and appointment reminders. WatchText handles between-visit client wellness monitoring — a different trigger type (daily safety check vs. appointment reminder) and different operational pain (emergency prevention vs. scheduling efficiency).*

**Evidence:**
- Home care agencies face a major liability gap between scheduled visits: if a client falls or has a health event, the agency may not know for hours or days. Manual daily phone check-ins are labor-intensive and inconsistently done. Automated SMS check-in at near-zero marginal cost closes this gap.
- The "between-visit monitoring" use case is underserved by current tools — CareZone, Life Alert, and consumer check-in apps target families directly (B2C); no purpose-built B2B SMS check-in tool for home care agencies exists.
- Market: 33,000+ licensed home health and companion care agencies. Aging-in-place market growing at 7.2% CAGR. Every agency with active clients has this liability exposure.
- AI angle: AI pattern analysis on response data (time-of-day, response latency, keyword variance) over days/weeks — not just a dumb SMS ping. First product in the portfolio to apply AI inference to the response signal itself, not just the message trigger.
- TCPA: existing client/agency relationship = established business relationship; standard care agreement includes communication consent.
- Dual trigger: (1) missed_call from adult child or prospective client inquiring about home care; (2) reminder_due for daily wellness check-in schedule.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. $99–149/mo for a 20-client agency is trivial vs. liability and staffing cost savings. Large TAM (33,000+ agencies). No dominant daily-check-in SMS tool for the home care agency B2B segment.
- Popularity Potential: **7.5** — medium/high confidence. Aging-in-place and family peace-of-mind is a cultural mega-trend. NAHC, HCA (Home Care Association of America), state home care associations — organized, directory-accessible. "We use AI to check on your loved one every morning" is a powerful client-acquisition differentiator → agency word-of-mouth very strong.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **7.5** — high confidence. Daily check-in = recurring reminder; SAFE/HELP/STOP keywords simple enough for elderly SMS users; AI escalation rule = configurable no-response threshold; onboard in under 10 minutes.

**Composite:** (7.0 × 0.40) + (7.5 × 0.35) + (7.5 × 0.25) = 2.800 + 2.625 + 1.875 = **7.300**

**Evidence:**
- Assisted living communities schedule 20–40+ weekly activities. Resident participation rates are a quality-of-life and marketing metric — communities advertise engagement rates to prospective families choosing a facility.

- Market: 28,900+ assisted living communities + 5,500+ CCRCs (continuing care retirement communities) in the US. Independent operators (~60% of facilities) are faster buyers than national chains.
- TCPA: residents sign residency agreements with communication consent at move-in; simple opt-in documentation.
- Reachability: Argentum (formerly ALFA), LeadingAge — organized national associations with state chapter directories; annual conferences; facility management software vendor channels.
- "Safe connection" fit: keeping residents safely connected to family and to community life is the explicit mission of senior living activities programs.
- Decision-maker: activities director (department-level buyer) for smaller tools; may need executive director sign-off. Independent operator facilities move faster than chain-owned.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
