## Evidence Notes (sourced 2026-08-12)

- **All candidates:** decisive, sourced unit-economics evidence that reframes
  the whole day's investigation. White-glove/managed-service onboarding only
  pays for itself above roughly **$25K ACV** (a fully-loaded implementation
  specialist costs $135K-$165K; a CSM carries $110K-$180K OTE); the
  recommended hybrid breakpoints are **pure self-serve under $5K ACV**,
  pooled/assisted onboarding **$5K-$25K ACV**, dedicated white-glove **above
  $25K** ([pulserevops.com](https://pulserevops.com/revenue-architecture/ra0248),
  [command.ai](https://www.command.ai/blog/white-glove-vs-self-serve-onboarding-in-saas/)).
  Every current winner's ACV is roughly **$800-$1,450/year** (TextBack Local
  ~$948, NoShowGuard ~$828, SMSDispatch ~$1,440 blended) — an order of
  magnitude below even the $5K self-serve/assisted threshold. This **kills
  managed-service as a pricing model for any current winner outright**,
  including in bundled form (candidate B's combined ACV is still only
  ~$1,776/year). This finding wasn't obtained from generic reasoning; it's a
  sourced 2026-27 SaaS-economics benchmark directly disqualifying an entire
  model option the pool offers, and it retroactively confirms the token-lean
  self-serve build philosophy already documented in
  `docs/build-prompts/sms-core-engine.md` was the right call, not just a
  cost-saving guess.
- **A (TextBack Local usage-based):** no fresh pricing-elasticity evidence
  found for this specific switch. Usage-based (e.g. per-lead-captured)
  lowers the commitment barrier for a first-time buyer evaluating an
  unfamiliar vendor, which plausibly helps popularity, but caps upside
  revenue per high-volume account relative to flat subscription. Genuinely
  unresolved — carried at low confidence on both profit and popularity.
- **B (Local Biz SMS Suite):** the bundle logic is sound (salons, clinics,
  and contractors plausibly suffer both missed-call and no-show pain
  simultaneously), but today's sourced ACV math shows it doesn't move the
  onboarding-economics needle at all — the real motivation for testing a
  bundle (justifying assisted onboarding) doesn't hold up. Any profit
  benefit here would have to come from cross-sell efficiency alone, not
  from unlocking a higher-touch service tier.
- **C (SMSDispatch flat subscription):** works against SMSDispatch's own
  original evidence-based reasoning (2026-08-04: usage-based was chosen
  specifically because small-carrier load volume is seasonal and
  fragmented). A flat rate simplifies billing but likely reduces
  popularity for exactly the buyer segment usage-based was chosen to fit.
- **D (CallCatch Local usage-based):** the most interesting reframe. Voice
  AI carries real marginal telephony/speech-infra cost per call, unlike
  SMS — usage-based pricing passes that cost through instead of absorbing
  it as flat-fee margin risk, which is a better cost-alignment fit than the
  subscription model CallCatch Local currently uses. Doesn't close the
  composite gap with TextBack Local's SMS version, but is a legitimate
  pricing-model correction worth flagging for the existing launch kit.
