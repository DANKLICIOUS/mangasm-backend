## Evidence Notes (sourced 2026-08-14)

- **A (NoShowGuard usage-based):** no strong evidence either way. Unlike
  SMSDispatch's original usage-based rationale (seasonal, fragmented load
  volume), appointment-based local businesses generally have steadier,
  more predictable monthly volume, which is the classic case *for* flat
  subscription pricing rather than usage-based — this cuts against the
  switch, completing the pricing-sensitivity matrix with a negative result
  for all but the trucking/dispatch case.
- **B, C, D (privacy first, all three):** the sourced evidence this run is
  more tempered than most prior days' findings. Data minimization and
  consent management genuinely build buyer trust and reduce compliance
  risk, and shoppers increasingly "treat privacy as a brand test"
  ([cartbossblog.dweb.si](https://cartbossblog.dweb.si/data-privacy-in-marketing/)) —
  but the evidence frames this as **risk-reduction and compliance hygiene**,
  not a demand-generating differentiator that raises willingness to pay for
  trades or trucking-dispatch buyers, whose stated purchase trigger is
  speed-to-lead-recovery or load-confirmation reliability, not privacy
  positioning. Practically, `STOP`-based opt-out and consent capture were
  already specified as required, non-optional features of the shared engine
  in `docs/build-prompts/sms-core-engine.md` — meaning candidates B and C
  are less "new business ideas" than confirmation that baseline compliance
  work is already scoped, not a separate product to build.
- **D is the one exception worth flagging distinctly.** NoShowGuard's own
  launch kit already carries an open, unresolved TCPA-adjacent SMS-consent
  legal-review flag. Turning consent capture into a visible double opt-in
  *feature* (marketed as "we confirm your customer said yes" for
  appointment-based businesses — clinics especially, who carry their own
  compliance liability) is a case where privacy-first positioning directly
  answers an already-identified real risk rather than chasing a
  speculative differentiator. That's a materially different, more
  defensible case than B or C.
