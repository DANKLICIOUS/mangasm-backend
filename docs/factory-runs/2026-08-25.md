## Candidate Scorecards

### A — StudioText

**What it does:** Missed-call text-back and class/lesson reminder SMS for owner-operated dance, music, and art studios (10-200 students, single-location or small multi-location). Targets studios that rely on phone booking and manual class roster management without automated reminder tools.

**Evidence:**
- Dance/music/art studios have recurring weekly sessions (lessons and classes), creating a natural high-frequency reminder cadence — students may have 1-4 sessions per week, generating multiple reminder touchpoints per week per student.
- JackRabbit Technologies is the dominant studio management software for competitive dance studios (tuition management, recital tracking); however, JackRabbit focuses on competitive/performance studios and tuition billing — recreational studios (beginner classes, community music lessons, adult art classes) are underserved.
- No dominant SMS-specific missed-call tool exists for the studio vertical; general booking tools (Acuity, Calendly) send email reminders but SMS is an add-on, not native.
- Reachability: studio owner Facebook groups (Dance Studio Owner Association community, music studio owner groups) are active with 10k-50k members; same outreach pattern as grooming and home cleaning.
- TCPA: standard opt-in consent at enrollment; no industry-specific regulatory layer (not healthcare, not financial).
- Architecture: identical to existing SMS engine; lesson/class = appointment, instructor = staff, student/parent = client contact; keyword set CONFIRM/C/YES, CANCEL/R/NO, STOP → opt-out.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. Average studio revenue $150k-500k/year; $79/mo subscription is reasonable. Missed-call text-back directly recovers enrollment inquiries (lost caller = lost multi-month tuition enrollment, not just a single session). No-show rate for private lessons is commonly 10-15%.
- Popularity Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. Studio owner community is well-organized and active on social; clear product message ("never miss an enrollment call again") resonates with small business operators in this niche.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **8.0** — high confidence. Identical architecture to existing verticals; no integration with JackRabbit or other PMS needed for MVP; manual class schedule entry or CSV import; onboard under 10 minutes.

**Composite:** (7.0 × 0.40) + (7.0 × 0.35) + (8.0 × 0.25) = 2.80 + 2.45 + 2.00 = **7.250**

**Build gate check:**

### B — LeagueText

**What it does:** Game and practice schedule reminders + roster communication SMS for recreational sports leagues (adult softball, soccer, basketball, kickball leagues).

**Evidence:**
- Recreational sports leagues (adult leagues) use a mix of TeamSnap, SportsEngine, and GroupMe for team communication; game reminders and roster notifications are common features in these platforms.
- TeamSnap has strong penetration in recreational adult leagues; SportsEngine (NBC Sports subsidiary) targets youth club sports. The recreational adult league market has lower incumbent tool penetration than youth sports, but TeamSnap is well-known.
- Revenue per account concern: recreational leagues are often volunteer-run or run by parks and recreation departments — the decision-maker is not always a revenue-motivated small business owner. Private recreational league operators (bar leagues, social sports companies like ZogSports) are the viable buyer, but they are fewer in number.
- No fatal risk, but lower revenue-per-account and incumbent messaging app penetration limit the ceiling.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **6.5** — medium confidence. Private recreational league operators are a viable buyer; volunteer/parks and rec leagues are not. Market is smaller than studio or grooming.
- Popularity Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. Social media presence of league operators is strong; Facebook and Nextdoor are primary channels for adult league recruitment.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **8.0** — high confidence. Simple SMS broadcast + reminder architecture; group messaging is straightforward.

**Composite:** (6.5 × 0.40) + (7.0 × 0.35) + (8.0 × 0.25) = 2.60 + 2.45 + 2.00 = **7.050**

### C — YogaText

**What it does:** Class reminder and waitlist notification SMS for independent yoga and Pilates studios (not franchise/chain).

**Evidence:**
- Mindbody is the dominant booking and management platform for yoga and Pilates studios of all sizes — it includes class reminders (email and SMS) as built-in features, bundled at $129-349/mo.
- Studios already on Mindbody are not an addressable market for a standalone SMS reminder tool; the product would need to displace Mindbody or target studios that haven't adopted it.
- Non-Mindbody studios do exist (especially small sole-practitioner or 2-room studios priced out of Mindbody), but this is a smaller and harder-to-reach sub-segment.
- Composite near-miss: product is valid but Mindbody bundling substantially narrows the TAM.

### D — ChurchText

**What it does:** Event reminder and volunteer coordination SMS for faith communities (churches, synagogues, mosques).

**Evidence:**

- Willingness to pay is low: church communication tools (Planning Center, Breeze ChMS, Pushpay) are purchased by budget committees with long approval cycles; $79/mo subscription competes against free or heavily discounted nonprofit tiers from incumbents.
- The buyer (pastor, volunteer coordinator) is not an SMB operator with a P&L — they don't think in terms of revenue recovery from missed calls.

**Why it won:** StudioText has the highest ease-of-use score in this batch (identical architecture to existing verticals) and the strongest enrollment-recovery narrative — a missed call at a dance or music studio is a missed multi-month tuition enrollment, not just a single session. Must confirm that the MVP targets enrolled-student studios (dance recital programs, ongoing lesson series) rather than drop-in class operators.
