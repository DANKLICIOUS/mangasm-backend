## Design question for today

Two prior runs (08-02, 08-04) showed SMS beating voice AI head-to-head on the
*same* market/pain pair. Both were reactive ("missed calls"). Today tests
whether SMS's edge is specific to reactive missed-call recovery, or whether it
generalizes to a **proactive** use case (appointment reminders) and to a
**different pain class entirely** (lone-worker safety check-ins), where SMS
competes against fully-featured incumbent platforms rather than voice AI.

## Evidence Notes (sourced 2026-08-05)

- **D (NoShowGuard):** strong, directly relevant evidence. SMS appointment
  reminders/confirmations reduce no-shows by **25-58%** depending on industry
  and lead time; SMS open rates run **~98% vs ~20% for email**, and reminder
  sequences with a two-way confirm-or-reschedule step perform best. This is a
  **proactive** application (reminder before the appointment) rather than
  08-02/08-04's reactive missed-call recovery, and the evidence is at least as
  strong. Local service and appointment-based businesses (salons, clinics,
  contractors doing scheduled estimates) are the clearest buyer.
- **A (LoneCheck SMS):** real counter-signal, not a supporting case. SMS-based
  safety check-ins for lone/frontline workers already exist, but almost
  exclusively as **one feature within full lone-worker-safety platforms**
  (GPS/geofencing, panic button, fall detection, incident escalation, compliance
  reporting) sold as a bundled subscription — standalone SMS check-in is not a
  distinctly priced or purchased unit in this category. SMS here is a
  commodity feature of an incumbent product, not a wedge for a new one.
- **B (PetRemind):** no new pricing evidence sourced this run; carries forward
  the low-confidence compliance-training economics established 2026-07-29/
  08-01/08-04 (crowded incumbent field, no format-specific pricing precedent).
- **C (CommunityText):** same conflict-of-interest flag raised 2026-07-28 and
  2026-08-04 for privacy-first LGBTQ+ recommender concepts — duplicates
  Mangasm's own safety/community feature set rather than having an independent
  buyer. `buyer_clear` marked false again, consistent with prior runs.

## Winner — NoShowGuard · verdict: BUILD ✅ (composite 7.28, all 6 gates passed)

**Concept:** SMS appointment reminders and two-way confirmations for local
service businesses (salons, clinics, contractors, home-service estimates) —
an automatic text goes out ahead of a scheduled appointment ("Reminder: your
appointment is tomorrow at 2pm — reply C to confirm or R to reschedule"),
cutting no-shows and recovering the reschedule instead of losing the slot
silently. Same delivery mechanism and buyer profile as 08-02's TextBack
Local, but proactive (before the appointment) instead of reactive (after a
missed call).

**This is the FOURTH build-gate winner overall**, and the first to test SMS
outside a reactive missed-call context. It confirms SMS's edge **generalizes
to a second job-to-be-done** (no-show prevention, not just call recovery) at
an equal composite score (7.28, tying SMSDispatch) — while today's other
finding, LoneCheck SMS, shows the pattern is **not universal**: where SMS
check-in is already a bundled feature of a fully-featured incumbent platform
(lone-worker safety), it fails to clear the gate (5.17) because there's no
distinct buyer for a standalone version of a feature people already get
included elsewhere.

## Four-winner pattern — where SMS wins and where it doesn't

| Winner | Composite | Vertical | Delivery | Reactive/Proactive |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| CallCatch Local (07-28) | 7.08 | Home-services trades | Voice AI | Reactive |
| TextBack Local (08-02) | 7.40 | Home-services trades | **SMS** | Reactive |
| SMSDispatch (08-04) | 7.28 | Trucking dispatch | **SMS** | Reactive |
| NoShowGuard (08-05) | 7.28 | Appointment-based local biz | **SMS** | **Proactive** |

Refined conclusion: SMS wins whenever the underlying job is a **discrete,
time-boxed customer-contact event that a small/local business currently
handles by phone or nothing at all** — regardless of whether the trigger is
reactive (missed call) or proactive (upcoming appointment). It does **not**
win by default in categories where SMS is already commoditized inside an
existing full-platform incumbent (today's LoneCheck SMS finding, mirroring
04's compliance-training finding for B). The Ecosystem Builder's reusable
core engine should stay SMS-first for local-service verticals; the filter
for a *new* vertical going forward is "is SMS already bundled into what this
buyer uses today," not just "is SMS cheaper to build than voice."
