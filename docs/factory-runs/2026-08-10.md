## Evidence Notes (sourced 2026-08-10)

- **A (SmartRemind):** strong, directly relevant evidence, and the most
  useful pull of the run. A personalized, multi-channel reminder strategy
  (matching SMS/email/voice to each customer's actual channel preference,
  plus a well-timed second reminder) achieves **~40% no-show reduction**,
  versus a **34% weighted-mean reduction from a single generic reminder**
  across 31 randomized controlled trials; a second, well-timed reminder adds
  another **10-20%** on top of the first
  ([getprosper.ai](https://www.getprosper.ai/blog/appointment-reminders-reduce-no-shows-guide),
  [noterro.com](https://www.noterro.com/blog/reduce-no-shows-with-personalize-automated-reminders)).
  This is a real, sourced lift over NoShowGuard's (2026-08-05) flat
  single-SMS approach — but it comes at a real engineering cost: supporting
  email/voice channels and per-customer preference storage is materially
  more scope than one SMS template, which is exactly why Ease scores lower
  here (6.5) than NoShowGuard's (8.0) despite the stronger profit case.
- **B (ShiftCircles):** real counter-signal, not supporting evidence. The
  literature does **not** show pure in-person huddles outperforming digital
  care-coordination tools — if anything, virtual/electronic huddles show
  measurably better team participation and patient-experience scores than
  static in-person ones, and the strongest-performing pattern found is a
  **hybrid** (digital handoff reinforced by direct communication), not an
  in-person-only managed service
  ([oncologynurseadvisor.com](https://www.oncologynurseadvisor.com/features/patient-care-coordination-electronic-nursing-huddles/),
  [ojin.nursingworld.org](https://ojin.nursingworld.org/table-of-contents/volume-30-2025/number-1-january-2025/articles-on-previously-published-topics/virtual-huddles-improving-healthcare-team-participation-and-patient-experience/)).
  Scored down accordingly across all three categories, not just discounted
  for lack of data — this is evidence actively against the mechanism.
- **C (PetTrain Meetups):** no fresh search this run; carries forward the
  low-confidence compliance-training economics established repeatedly since
  2026-07-29 (crowded field, no format-specific pricing precedent). One
  plausible but unverified positive: hands-on skills (safe animal handling)
  may fit in-person format better than remote delivery — flagged as an
  assumption, not evidence, since nothing was sourced to confirm it this run.
- **D (PetCare Bites):** no fresh search this run; carries forward general
  microlearning completion-rate evidence from 2026-08-06 at a steep
  discount, since the buyer here is an individual consumer rather than an
  employer with a training budget. Real structural problem: pet owners have
  abundant free care-coordination content already available (vet websites,
  breed-specific forums, video content), undermining willingness to pay for
  a subscription product covering the same ground. `buyer_clear` marked
  false.

## Winner — SmartRemind · verdict: VALIDATE (composite 6.72, gate NOT passed)

**Concept:** personalized, multi-channel appointment reminders (SMS, email,
or voice matched to each customer's actual response history) plus a
well-timed second reminder, for the same local-service-business buyer as
NoShowGuard. The evidence is genuinely the strongest sourced this run and
supports a real performance edge over flat single-channel SMS — but it fails
`composite_at_least_7` by a narrow margin (6.72), driven entirely by Ease:
multi-channel integration and preference storage is real added build scope
that a single-SMS product doesn't carry.

**This is the closest non-winner to date and a legitimate v2 roadmap item,
not a rejected concept.** The honest read: don't build SmartRemind as a
separate product — ship NoShowGuard's flat-SMS MVP first (per its existing
launch kit and build prompt), validate it with a real pilot, and treat
channel-personalization as the natural paid-tier upgrade once there's
revenue to justify the extra integration work. This is consistent with the
token-lean build philosophy already documented in
`docs/build-prompts/sms-core-engine.md` — add scope after paying customers
exist, not before.
