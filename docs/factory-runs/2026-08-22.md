## Candidate Scorecards

### A — VetText

**What it does:** Missed-call text-back + appointment reminder sequence for independent veterinary clinics (1-3 vets, owner-operated or single-location practices).

**Evidence:**
- Vet clinics have documented no-show rates of 10-15% with per-visit revenue of $150-400; a single recovered no-show covers months of subscription cost — industry commonly discussed in AVMA Practice Management resources.
- Dominant scheduling software (Cornerstone, AVImark, ezyVet) is expensive ($300-700/mo for practice management) and targets multi-vet hospitals; independent clinics frequently run on manual phone booking without automated reminders.
- "Missed call at vet clinic" returns active complaint threads on Reddit and Yelp reviews — clear consumer-side signal.
- TCPA/HIPAA note: veterinary practices are NOT covered entities under HIPAA (animal medical records are not protected PHI); standard TCPA SMS consent applies — no additional regulatory burden beyond existing verticals.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **8.0** — high confidence. $150+ per recovered no-show makes $99/mo an easy ROI story; market is 30,000+ independent vet clinics in the US; no dominant SMS-specific tool in this niche.
- Popularity Potential: **7.5** — medium confidence. Active owner-operator community (AVMA, VetPartners); reachable via LinkedIn and Facebook groups for clinic owners; clear buyer pain that maps directly to the product.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **8.0** — high confidence. Same architecture as TextBack Local; keyword set: CONFIRM/C/YES → confirmed, CANCEL/R/NO → declined, STOP → opt-out; no vet software API needed for MVP (manual appointment entry); onboard in under 10 minutes.

**Composite:** (8.0 × 0.40) + (7.5 × 0.35) + (8.0 × 0.25) = 3.20 + 2.625 + 2.00 = **7.825**

**Build gate check:**
- Composite ≥ 7.0: ✅ (7.825)

- ≥ 2 medium/high confidence: ✅ (Profit high, Ease high, Popularity medium)

### B — GroomText

**What it does:** Missed-call text-back + appointment reminder for independent dog grooming salons (1-3 groomers, owner-operated).

**Evidence:**
- Grooming salons report high no-show/late-cancel rates (commonly 15-20% in grooming community forums on Facebook); at $50-80/groom, 4 no-shows/day = $200-320 in wasted slot revenue.
- StyleSeat and Booksy serve salons (hair, nail, beauty); neither markets specifically to pet grooming as a primary vertical — the operator UI is generic.
- Groomer-specific tools (Gingr, 123Pet) exist but are routing/booking focused; none emphasize missed-call text-back as a primary feature.
- Reachability: groomer-specific Facebook groups (Dog Groomers group: 100k+ members) are active and known for "what software do you use" conversations — same outreach pattern as cleaning businesses.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**

- Ease of Use/Learning: **8.5** — high confidence. Identical architecture to existing verticals; single-trigger for MVP (missed-call text-back); reminder sequence optional in phase 2.

### C — BoardText

**What it does:** Check-in and check-out reminder SMS for pet boarding and day-care facilities.

**Evidence:**
- Pet boarding no-shows are structurally different from grooming/vet no-shows: a customer who forgets to pick up their dog is a service continuation problem, not a revenue slot loss in the same way — the dog is still there eating and occupying a kennel run.
- The acute pain is communication around drop-off/pick-up times and emergency contact, not reminder conversion. This is less a missed-call/reminder product and more a logistics communication tool — different product shape.
- Boarding-specific software (Gingr, Kennel Booker, PetExec) handles reservations natively; the pain of missed calls at boarding is lower because customers book well in advance.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **6.5** — medium confidence. Real market but weaker revenue-recovery narrative; pricing ceiling lower ($49/mo).
- Popularity Potential: **6.0** — low-medium confidence. Less acute pain; boarding software incumbent penetration higher.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **7.5** — high confidence. Simple SMS architecture.

**Composite:** (6.5 × 0.40) + (6.0 × 0.35) + (7.5 × 0.25) = 2.60 + 2.10 + 1.875 = **6.575**

**Build gate check:**
- Composite ≥ 7.0: ❌ (6.575)
- **GATE: FAILED ❌** — weaker revenue-recovery narrative and incumbent penetration in boarding software.

### D — PetScript

**What it does:** Prescription refill and medication reminder SMS for veterinary clinic pharmacies or pet pharmacies.

**Evidence:**
- Veterinary prescription reminders are handled by the dispensing pharmacy system (VetRxDirect, Chewy Pharmacy) or the clinic's own PMS; neither is a gap that a standalone SMS product fills cleanly.
- Medication adherence SMS for pets sits at the intersection of pharmacy and medical compliance — both regulated sectors even if vet clinics are not HIPAA-covered; state pharmacy board rules govern dispensing reminders.
- Founder complexity: must partner with or work around existing pharmacy dispensing systems; solo founder can't build the integration surface without significant technical effort.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **6.0** — low confidence. Niche within a niche; pharmacy system partnerships required.
- Popularity Potential: **5.5** — low confidence. Existing prescription management tools already send refill reminders.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **6.0** — medium confidence. Regulatory friction and pharmacy system integration reduce simplicity.

**Composite:** (6.0 × 0.40) + (5.5 × 0.35) + (6.0 × 0.25) = 2.40 + 1.925 + 1.50 = **5.825**

**Build gate check:**
- Composite ≥ 7.0: ❌ (5.825)
- fatal_risk: ⚠️ regulatory complexity (pharmacy dispensing rules) + incumbent integration requirement
- **GATE: FAILED ❌**
