## Candidate Scorecards

### A — CareText

**What it does:** Missed-call text-back and visit reminder SMS for owner-operated non-medical home care agencies (personal care, companion care, IHSS, private-pay). Targets agencies with 10-50 active caregivers that rely on phone booking and manual scheduling.

**Evidence:**
- Non-medical home care (personal care, companion care) is NOT covered by HIPAA — these agencies are not covered entities or business associates; standard TCPA consent applies.
- Dominant scheduling software targets large agencies: WellSky, Alayacare, ClearCare (acquired by WellSky) price for 50+ caregiver organizations; small SMB agencies run on Excel, Google Sheets, or basic scheduling apps with no automated missed-call SMS.
- Aging-in-place tailwind: US home care market growing 5-8% annually; 30,000+ home care agencies nationwide; independent/family-owned segment is the largest segment by count but smallest by software tool penetration.
- Visit confirmation is high-pain: caregiver no-shows cause client escalations and state compliance issues (many states require a minimum visit completion rate for Medicaid waiver participation — though non-medical private-pay agencies are less exposed to this).
- Architecture: identical to CleanText (visit = appointment, caregiver = service provider, client = customer); keyword set CONFIRM/C/YES → confirmed, CANCEL/R/NO → declined, STOP → opt-out.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **7.5** — medium confidence. Agencies pay $200-500/mo for scheduling software at scale; $99/mo SMS add-on is an easy anchor. High recurring revenue per account; caregiver churn creates constant scheduling pressure (strong product stickiness).
- Popularity Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. Reachable via home care association channels (HCAOA, PHI); Facebook groups for home care agency owners are active. Word-of-mouth within the agency owner community is strong.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **7.0** — high confidence. Same architecture as CleanText; no integration with scheduling software required for MVP (manual visit entry or CSV import); onboard in under 10 minutes.

**Composite:** (7.5 × 0.40) + (7.0 × 0.35) + (7.0 × 0.25) = 3.00 + 2.45 + 1.75 = **7.200**

**Build gate check:**

- buyer_clear: ✅ (home care agency owner/operator, 10-50 caregiver headcount)

### B — PediatricText

**What it does:** Missed-call text-back and appointment reminder for ABA therapy, speech therapy, and occupational therapy clinics serving pediatric populations.

**Evidence:**
- ABA/speech/OT clinics have significant no-show rates (commonly cited at 10-20% for pediatric therapy); at $150-250/session, even a few recovered sessions per week makes the ROI case.
- HIPAA exposure: therapy clinics treating minors are covered entities; sending appointment reminder SMS requires a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement (BAA). BAA is manageable for an established SaaS vendor but adds setup friction at MVP stage — solo founder must execute BAA before first client.
- Existing tools: SimplePractice, Therapy Brands, Kareo all include appointment reminders as bundled features for therapy practices; the gap is smaller than in non-medical verticals.
- Second-place result: passes build gate with caveats, not packaged this run — CareText takes priority given cleaner regulatory profile.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **7.5** — medium confidence. High per-session revenue makes ROI case strong; therapy clinic owners are accustomed to paying for practice management software.
- Popularity Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. Reachable via therapy association channels (AOTA, ASHA, ABAI); therapy clinic owner Facebook groups are active.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **6.5** — medium confidence. BAA requirement adds onboarding friction; existing tool bundling means product must differentiate clearly.

**Composite:** (7.5 × 0.40) + (7.0 × 0.35) + (6.5 × 0.25) = 3.00 + 2.45 + 1.625 = **7.075**

### C — RideText

**What it does:** Trip confirmation and pickup reminder SMS for non-emergency medical transportation (NEMT) providers.

**Evidence:**
- NEMT providers primarily serve Medicaid-funded trips; trip scheduling is controlled by Medicaid managed care plans or NEMT brokers (MTM, Modivcare, LogistiCare) — the provider rarely controls their own booking calendar.
- The broker-controlled dispatch model means the NEMT provider cannot unilaterally add SMS reminders to their workflow; the broker's system governs trip confirmation, not the provider.
- Private-pay NEMT (non-Medicaid) is a small slice of the market and would be the only viable target — but these operators are very small and price-sensitive.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **6.5** — medium confidence. Private-pay NEMT is small; Medicaid-funded NEMT is broker-controlled (product can't reach that segment).
- Popularity Potential: **6.0** — low-medium confidence. Medicaid NEMT operators won't pay for a tool they can't deploy in their core workflow; private-pay operators are too few.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **6.5** — medium confidence. SMS architecture is simple, but the broker dependency limits onboarding to a narrow subset of providers.

**Composite:** (6.5 × 0.40) + (6.0 × 0.35) + (6.5 × 0.25) = 2.60 + 2.10 + 1.625 = **6.325**

**Build gate check:**
- Composite ≥ 7.0: ❌ (6.325)
- **GATE: FAILED ❌** — government contract dependency; broker-controlled dispatch limits product applicability.

## Decision

**Winner:** CareText (Missed-Call Text-Back + Visit Reminder for Non-Medical Home Care Agencies)  
**Composite Score:** 7.200  
**Decision:** BUILD ✅ — 11th build-gate winner  
**Why it won:** CareText has the cleanest regulatory profile in the care coordination space (non-medical = not HIPAA-covered) combined with a large, fragmented, underserved SMB market. Architecture is identical to CleanText — no new code for MVP. High caregiver turnover in home care creates persistent scheduling pressure, which means the product stays valuable beyond the initial onboarding period.

**Strongest unresolved assumption:** State-by-state variation in home care licensure — some states require home care agencies to be licensed as Home Health Agencies (which may bring HIPAA into scope if they accept Medicare/Medicaid). Must confirm that the target segment is private-pay companion care only, not Medicaid waiver providers, before outreach.

**PediatricText note:** Second-place at 7.075, also passes the build gate. Worth packaging after CareText validates — BAA template is the primary additional overhead vs. CareText.
