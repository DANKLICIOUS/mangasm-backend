## Candidate Scorecards

### A — PitchLearn

**What it does:** AI sales enablement microlearning for SMB sales teams. AI ingests the company's own sales collateral — pitch decks, CRM loss reason notes, G2 competitor reviews, release notes — and generates personalized daily 2-minute bite-sized lessons delivered via Slack or SMS. Individual quiz scores surface to the manager, revealing which reps have knowledge gaps on which topics before those gaps cost deals.

**Evidence:**
- Sales enablement is a $2.6B market (Gartner) growing at 19% CAGR — dominated by Highspot/Seismic/Showpad at $50K+/year enterprise contracts. No AI-native microlearning tool for SMB sales teams (25-200 reps) exists at the $400-800/month price point.
- Documented problem: New reps forget 70% of product training within a week (assumption; widely cited in sales enablement research). Managers can't diagnose knowledge gaps without a quiz signal.
- Buyer: VP of Sales or Head of Sales at a 25-200 person B2B company. This buyer has real budget, feels the pain in win-rate numbers, and is active in Revenue Collective (Pavilion) peer networks.
- Pricing: $20-40/seat/month × 20-rep team = $400-800/month = $4,800-9,600/year ARR per customer.
- Reachability: Revenue Collective/Pavilion, LinkedIn VP Sales persona, G2 user communities, cold email to VP Sales with a "free 3-day audit of your team's product knowledge" hook.
- AI-native alignment: Strongest in today's run — AI generates all content from customer-specific collateral; no content team or curriculum designer needed by the buyer.
- No regulated content, no HIPAA, no special data concerns beyond standard employment data.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **7.5** — medium/high confidence. Per-seat SaaS in a large, funded market; SMB gap is documented; AI differentiator eliminates the buyer's biggest objection (content creation cost). Positive: Win-rate improvement is measurable → easy ROI case. Counter: SMB sales tech is crowded; VP Sales sees 10+ pitches per quarter; churn risk if team shrinks.
- Popularity Potential: **7.5** — medium/high confidence. Sales leaders are the most active B2B buyers on LinkedIn and in peer communities. "AI turns your pitch deck into daily training" is memorable. Positive: Revenue Collective is organized and influential; VP Sales share tools loudly when they work. Counter: Buyer fatigue in sales enablement; category skepticism is high.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **8.0** — high confidence. Daily 2-min lesson in Slack (existing tool); AI generates content from uploaded docs (no new workflow); quiz score dashboard is minimal; time to first lesson under 10 minutes from signup. Positive: No new app for reps; value visible in day 1 (quiz scores). Counter: AI lesson quality depends on source material quality — poor pitch decks produce weak lessons.

### B — OnboardLearn

**What it does:** AI new-hire onboarding microlearning for SMBs. Founder or HR manager connects existing docs (Google Drive, Notion, Confluence, employee handbook PDFs) → AI reads them and auto-generates a personalized 30-day microlearning curriculum per role → new hire receives daily 5-min lessons via Slack or email → manager dashboard shows completion and quiz scores. No content creation required by the buyer.

**Evidence:**
- Only 12% of US employees say their company does onboarding well (Gallup — sourced). Poor onboarding correlates with high 90-day attrition — a costly, measurable problem.
- Market: 6M+ US businesses with 10-200 employees. Ideal segment: 25-150 employees, growing team, no dedicated L&D function.
- Current alternatives: Trainual ($49-299/mo) requires manual content creation — no AI. BambooHR is a full HRIS with basic onboarding tasks but no microlearning. No AI-native "reads your docs, creates the training" product exists for SMBs at sub-$500/month.
- Pricing: $199-399/month unlimited new hires, or $99/month + $19/new hire. A 50-person company = trivial cost vs. 90-day attrition.
- Reachability: SHRM, HR Brew, LinkedIn HR persona, Notion and Google Workspace partner channels, VC-backed startup HR communities.
- AI-native alignment: ✅ genuine — AI document parsing + curriculum generation is the entire product differentiator; without AI, this is just another LMS.

- Popularity Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. SHRM and LinkedIn HR communities are organized; high-growth startups are early adopters and share tools in peer networks. Positive: "AI reads your handbook, creates the training" is a shareable hook with a clear before/after. Counter: HR buyers are overwhelmed with tools; adding another integration has friction even when value is clear.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **8.0** — high confidence. Connect docs → AI generates → new hire gets daily lessons. No new tool for the new hire. Manager sees completion dashboard. Time to first lesson: under 10 minutes. Positive: "No content creation" removes the #1 barrier to LMS adoption. Counter: AI quality depends on documentation quality — disorganized docs = weak lessons.

### C — BiteLearn

**Evidence:**
- IFA (International Franchise Association) surveys consistently rank training consistency as a top franchisee challenge. Franchise systems that allow location-level variation in training experience brand quality drift and increased liability exposure.
- Market: ~800,000 franchise locations across ~3,000 US franchise systems. Ideal segment: mid-size systems with 50-500 locations — large enough to have a training director with budget, small enough to move without enterprise procurement.
- Pricing: $5-10/location/month × 100 locations = $6,000-12,000/year ARR per franchise system. With 100 chains: $600K-$1.2M ARR.
- Alternatives: Wisetail, Trainual, Toast Training — general LMS or video tools, not franchise-specific with AI adaptation. No AI-native franchise microlearning competitor identified.
- Reachability: IFA annual conference, franchise development consultants, franchisor peer groups.
- AI-native alignment: ✅ genuine — AI generates location-specific content variants based on location performance data (mystery shop scores, customer review sentiment); not just static content delivery.

Positive: Franchise systems have recurring training budgets; contract value scales with location count. Counter: Sales cycles to franchise systems can be 3-6 months; training director role exists mainly at 50+ location systems.

- Ease of Use/Learning: **7.5** — high confidence. Positive: Fits in a shift-change briefing; no new tool for staff. Counter: Franchisor must provide source training materials; if those are disorganized, AI content quality suffers.

### D — CertLearn

**What it does:** AI-personalized continuing education (CE) microlearning for licensed professionals (real estate agents, insurance agents, cosmetologists, social workers). Breaks required CE credit courses into 5-min daily modules; AI adapts sequencing based on quiz scores; tracks progress toward renewal deadline and nudges via SMS.

**Evidence:**
- Large addressable market: ~2M NAR real estate agents (15-45 CE hours/2-year cycle), ~500K insurance agents (24 hrs/2-year), ~1M cosmetologists (8-16 hrs/year), plus social workers, therapists, and others.
- Pain is real: Current CE options are boring multi-hour video courses completed at 2x speed. No personalization, no adaptive learning, no engagement.
- Regulatory barrier: CE credits must be approved by the relevant state licensing board for each profession/state combination. Pursuing approvals is a multi-month process requiring curriculum review and fees for each state × profession pair. This delays monetization significantly — professionals won't pay for content that doesn't count toward their CE requirement.
- Revenue model: $79-129/year subscription per professional or $19-29/CE credit package. B2C or employer-paid benefit.
- Cyclic demand: Professionals engage only near their renewal deadline; engagement is near-zero outside the window. High seasonal churn risk.

**Scores (raw, before weighting):**
- Profit Potential: **6.5** — medium confidence. Large addressable market, but CE credit approval is a regulatory gate that delays time-to-revenue by 6-18 months per state/profession. Positive: Once approved, subscription sticks through renewal cycles. Counter: Regulatory overhead = significant capital requirement before earning credited revenue; revenue is seasonal, not recurring.
- Popularity Potential: **7.0** — medium confidence. NAR and insurance association channels are organized. Positive: "AI that makes CE actually interesting" has a clear pain-to-solution narrative. Counter: Demand is cyclic; off-cycle engagement is near-zero.
- Ease of Use/Learning: **7.5** — high confidence. Daily 5-min lesson via SMS; no new app; AI tracks progress and deadline; time to value is immediate. Positive: "5 minutes a day toward your CE requirement" is a clear hook. Counter: Legitimacy requires CE approval — without it, professionals have no reason to pay.

**Composite:** (6.5 × 0.40) + (7.0 × 0.35) + (7.5 × 0.25) = 2.600 + 2.450 + 1.875 = **6.925**

**Build gate check:**
- Composite ≥ 7.0: ❌ (6.925 — near-miss; profit depressed by regulatory approval overhead)
- No category < 6.0: ✅
- ≥ 2 medium/high confidence: ✅
- buyer_clear: ✅ (licensed professional — individual subscription)
- validation_feasible: ✅ (survey 20 real estate agents: "Would you pay if it earned CE credit?")
- fatal_risk: ✅ no outright fatal risk, but regulatory approval overhead is a material execution barrier
- **GATE: FAILED ❌** (composite below 7.0; regulatory approval delays revenue; cyclic demand pattern reduces retention)
