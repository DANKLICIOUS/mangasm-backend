## Evidence Notes (sourced 2026-08-21)

- **A (PoolText):** Pool service companies run recurring weekly or biweekly visits, with residential
  contracts typically priced at $100–300/month. A missed service visit generates immediate customer
  complaints (untreated water is an urgent homeowner problem) and puts the full contract value at
  risk — at $200/month, losing one customer from a missed-visit chain costs $2,400/year. The pool
  service software market is led by Skimmer (pool-specific) with secondary coverage from general
  field-service platforms (Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro). Critically: Skimmer's product focus
  is operational — route management, chemical logging, invoicing, and customer service records. Its
  client communication features are email-centric and do not include automated missed-call text-back
  as a marketed feature. This distinguishes pool service from gym booking (Mindbody bundles SMS
  reminders as a core product) and salon booking (Booksy/Vagaro bundle SMS reminders as a core
  product): Skimmer is an operational management tool, not a booking-and-reminder platform. The
  general field-service platforms (Jobber) do include client reminders in higher tiers, creating
  some bundling risk for pool companies that use Jobber — but pool-specific adoption of Jobber is
  lower than landscaping/lawn-care adoption. Evidence quality: **medium** — the structural case
  is strong (recurring-visit business, real contract-loss economics, Skimmer's operational vs.
  communication positioning), but no directly cited percentage for missed-visit rates or
  "missed-call text-back specifically as the conversion differentiator" quote for pool companies.

- **B (TutorText):** Private tutors and small tutoring centers face genuine SMS-relevant pain: a
  missed $80–150/hour session is a direct revenue loss, and parents routinely ignore email but
  respond to SMS. The tutoring software landscape is meaningfully fragmented — TutorCruncher,
  Teachworks, Tutorbird, Acuity, and many tutors using no dedicated software — with no single
  Mindbody/Booksy-class incumbent controlling >50% of market. Missed-call text-back framing for
  tutoring is honest as inferred product-market fit (a parent calling a tutoring center that's
  busy, receiving no callback, and signing up with a competitor instead), but the specific framing
  — "missed-call text-back as the conversion differentiator for tutors" — is not sourced this
  run. General SMS reminder evidence (98% open rate, 20-40% no-show reduction) applies directly.
  The near-miss pattern from BizFitText's 6.95 composite should not artificially inflate TutorText
  to clear a gate; the evidence grade is honestly **medium across all categories** with the
  text-back-specific framing unsourced.

- **C (LawnText):** Jobber is the recognized dominant field-service management platform for
  landscaping and lawn-care companies, and Jobber explicitly markets "automated client reminders"
  and "two-way texting" as product features on its Growth tier. This is the same failure pattern
  seen with FitText (Mindbody bundles reminders) and SalonText (Booksy/Vagaro bundle reminders):
  the feature a standalone tool would sell already ships inside the dominant incumbent used by
  the target buyer. Additionally, lawn/landscaping service windows are less time-precise than
  cleaning or pool visits ("we'll be there Tuesday morning" vs. a 9am slot), reducing the
  urgency of a missed-call text-back loop relative to fixed-appointment verticals. LawnText
  correctly fails on popularity: **low confidence** that a standalone SMS text-back tool
  carves out space alongside Jobber's built-in communication features for this vertical.

- **D (ShiftText):** Frontline workforce shift reminder SMS faces immediate competition from
  scheduling platforms (When I Work, Deputy, Homebase) that include SMS-based shift reminders,
  push notifications, and schedule-change alerts as core features with strong SMB adoption in
  retail, restaurants, and hospitality. Beyond the bundling problem, the product framing
  misaligns with the existing engine: "missed-call text-back" doesn't map to the shift-reminder
  use case — shift workers aren't calling in inquiries, and the business is pushing outbound
  schedule notifications, not recovering inbound leads. Reframing as "shift confirmation SMS"
  competes directly with the core feature set of established platforms. **Low confidence across
  all categories; fatal_risk: framework mismatch + dominant incumbents.**

## Winner — PoolText · verdict: BUILD ✅ (composite 7.08, all 6 gates passed)

**Concept:** SMS-based missed-call text-back combined with service-visit confirmation and reminder
texts for independent and small residential pool/spa service companies — recovers a lost inquiry the
moment a call goes unanswered, then follows up with a 24-hour and 2-hour reminder sequence to cut
no-shows on booked service visits. Same core SMS engine and architecture as the other 7 winners
(`docs/build-prompts/sms-core-engine.md` applies directly).

**Gate check:**
- Composite ≥ 7.0: ✅ (7.08)
- No category < 6.0: ✅ (floor is 6.5 on Popularity)
- ≥ 2 medium/high confidence categories: ✅ (Profit: med, Popularity: med, Ease: high)
- buyer_clear: ✅ (independent pool service operators and small pool service companies, 1–10 technicians)
- validation_feasible: ✅ (same outreach path as TextBack Local and CleanText)
- fatal_risk: ✗ (no fatal risk; bundling risk is real but not dominant, per Skimmer's operational-not-booking positioning)

**This is the 8th build-gate winner overall.** Its composite (7.08) ties CallCatch Local (07-28)
for the lowest winning score and sits clearly below CleanText (7.48) — reflecting the honest
difference in evidence quality. PoolText's structural case is strong (recurring-visit economics,
contract-loss urgency, Skimmer's operational positioning leaving a gap for dedicated text-back),
but the evidence is inferential rather than directly cited. The win is legitimate and the gate
is passed, but the weaker evidence base relative to prior winners (especially CleanText and RezText)
is correctly reflected in the score.

**TutorText** is the strongest near-miss yet at 6.95 — one solid sourced statistic on tutoring
session no-show rates or a direct "missed-call text-back as the conversion differentiator for
tutoring centers" citation would push it cleanly over the gate next time. It is the most
actionable candidate in the near-miss pool.

**LawnText** confirms, for the third time this week, that the "reminder functionality bundled into a
dominant field-service incumbent" failure pattern (Jobber, as Mindbody was for gyms and Booksy/Vagaro
were for salons) reliably kills otherwise plausible candidates.

**ShiftText** closes out the "frontline teams" keyword for this engine: the product framing doesn't
map, and the incumbent platforms are strong. The frontline teams market keyword warrants a different
product structure (not missed-call text-back) if tested again.
